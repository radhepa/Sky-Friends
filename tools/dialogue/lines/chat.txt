## chat.wise

In the oldest story, the first Cirri wove their veils from the mist over a waterfall, and fell twice before they learned to trust them.
A heavy heart sinks a light village. That's why we sing every dusk: to set it down.
Each bell in the Ruined Belltowers was cast a little heavier than the last. That is how pride works. One more bell at a time.
The winds are like neighbours. Aure is kind, Sefa is lazy, Vell is a gossip and Borre is honest. A village needs all four.
My elders used to say: the sky gives to those who leave room. A full nest catches no breeze.
Count your dawns, the old saying goes, but count them on your fingers and not in your worries.
The first windkeepers set the Wind Stones before any walker asked for them. A welcome is best given before it's needed.
Never weave a veil in anger. It holds, but it carries you somewhere you didn't mean to go.
They say the Old Singers sang the first cloud, then sang a second so the first wouldn't be lonely. That's why clouds never come alone.

Rain falls past us all day long. The trick is to let it.

Patience is a cloud's whole art. It never hurries, and it crosses the world.

Be kind to the ground, walker. It is old and patient, and it has carried you all this way without once complaining.

Never trust a still day, they say. I'd add: never trust a still koi either. It's planning something.
